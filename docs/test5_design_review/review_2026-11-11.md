## 1. Seasons and the holdout

### What the rules say

- Test 5 trades Kalshi's price at the cutoff against nflverse's close as fair
  value, and is "labeled a ceiling".
- Test 6, as first written, is the one-shot 2025 holdout of the *model*
  against Kalshi candle prices at the cutoff, with a liquidity check first and
  ROI, CLV and calibration reported once.
- The Kill rule and Amendment 38 decision 7 / Amendment 40 decision 5: tests 6
  and 7 "use the Kalshi-versus-close approach only, designed by a later
  amendment". No market has model-based trading.
- The "Test 5 season range" ambiguity is open. Kalshi spread and total history
  starts 2025-08-21 (preseason), so 2025 is the first season it can cover.

### Ambiguities

1a. Is test 5 on 2025 the same test as test 6?
Under the kill rule, test 6's only strategy is Kalshi-versus-close. For 2025,
the only "close" that exists is nflverse's line, and the repo has no 2025
decision-time line (topic 9). So test 6 on 2025 can only be: nflverse's
2025 line as fair value against the Kalshi 2025 price at the cutoff. **That is
test 5 on 2025, line for line.** The only possible differences are labels
("ceiling" against "holdout") and the liquidity gate, which belongs to both.

1b. Does running test 5 on 2025 use up the holdout?
Yes, in substance. The holdout existed so that the 2025 season would be seen
once, by a strategy fixed beforehand. Once any number combining 2025 Kalshi
prices with 2025 nflverse lines has been seen, every later choice (the
threshold, sigma, the price rule, the liquidity filter) can be tuned to it.
Any later 2025 test of the same idea would be judged on a seen season, which
the Discipline section counts as a new version. The model's own holdout use
(Amendment 26 decision 12, Amendment 37 decision 3) is gone either way,
because the model does not trade.

1c. Which games inside a season.
Version 1 is the model's scope, but the cutoff rule was validated only for
Thursday, Sunday and Monday non-neutral regular-season games (Amendment 4).
Test 5 has no injury inputs, so that reason does not apply to it directly.
Still, nothing in the rules defines a cutoff for the other games, and Amendment
4 lists them as excluded.

1d. Whether the version 1 rule (Amendments 1, 3, 4) carries over to 2025 and
2026. It is defined over 2010 to 2024; the code (features/game_times.py,
SEASONS = 2010 to 2024) builds nothing for 2025.

### Options for which games test 5 uses

| Option | Games (from local files) | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. All of 2025, version 1 games, run once, declared to be test 6's one-shot holdout as well | 258 games, so at most 516 game-markets (spread, total) | Uses the only full Kalshi season. One pre-registered run, no tuning. Matches what test 6 must be under the kill rule anyway. | No untouched season left for the Kalshi-versus-close idea. Below "a few hundred" bets is possible (topic 6). |
| B. 2025 split: weeks 1 to 9 as test 5 (may tune), weeks 10 to 18 as test 6 (one shot) | 130 / 128 games, so at most 260 / 256 game-markets | Keeps a formal holdout. Lets the threshold or price rule be set on seen data. | Each half is under or near "a few hundred". Early and late season differ (liquidity grows; line quality changes). Tuning on 130 games is mostly noise. |
| C. 2026 to date from our own collector | 87 Kalshi games with game date up to 2026-11-09. Only 12 of them have an nflverse line locally (the schedules file stops at 2026-10-04); the rest need an nflverse refresh. 14 games (all on 2026-10-04) have no snapshot before their cutoff. | No backfill needed for prices. Our own rows were checked against candles (data_sources section 4). | Small. The collector keeps only fee-inclusive asks, so there are no bids, no volume and no liquidity gate. The last snapshot is never within 60 minutes of the cutoff (topic 4). Overlaps the test 7 period. |
| D. 2025 (A) plus 2026 to date as a second, reported-only block | 258 + up to 87 | More data. | Mixes two price sources (candles against our snapshots) and two cutoff gaps. Needs the refresh. Adds a second reading, so a second chance to find something. |

**Recommendation: A.** Settle by amendment that:

- test 5 runs once on the 258 version 1 games of 2025;
- under the kill rule, that run is also test 6's one-shot holdout, so test 6
  is not run again on 2025;
- every parameter (fair-value form, sigma, w, price rule, fee, bet rule,
  threshold, liquidity filter, minimum bet count) is fixed before the
  backfill's prices are joined to any line.

Reason: test 6 cannot differ from test 5 on 2025 without a decision-time line
that does not exist. A split (B) gives two readings that are each too small.
The approach has almost nothing to tune if the parameters come from 2018 to
2024 (topic 3). Test 7 then becomes the only out-of-sample check, on 2026
games after the freeze (topic 9).

treat as fair value, and in 2025 they exist only in the playoffs, which are
outside version 1.

**Recommendation: A.** Spread and total are the markets test 5's own words
("nflverse's close") give a fair value for. ML would add correlated bets, not
independent ones. First-half and ML markets are left to test 7's forward data
under the Discipline section's first-half rule, if they are kept at all.

## 3. Fair value from the close

### The mapping (fixed by docs/data_sources.md section 1 and verified fixtures)

- Home strike "HOME wins by over X" is YES if and only if result > X.
- Away strike "AWAY wins by over X" is YES if and only if -result > X.
- Total strike is YES if and only if total > X.
- X = floor_strike, always X.5 in every local row (302,182 rows). Spread
  strikes start at 1.5, so no strike is "wins by over 0.5".
- The ticker suffix is X rounded up (TB8 has floor_strike 7.5; TOTAL-...-69
  has 68.5, from the fixtures).
- A Kalshi strike can never push. A whole-number nflverse line can push, but
  only for a sportsbook bet, and test 5 never prices a sportsbook bet. The line
  only sets the centre of the distribution.

### Ambiguities

3a. Which residuals give sigma. Test 5 has no model, so Amendment 26 decision
16's "out-of-sample model residuals" has no direct analogue. The line's own
residuals (actual minus line) are the natural replacement, and they never
touch a test season if taken from before 2025.

3b. Window for sigma and w. Amendment 26 uses 2013 to T-1 for w and the model's
own seasons (2018 to T-1) for sigma. For T = 2025 that means w from 2013 to
2024; only 2018 to 2024 was computed here, as the request allows.

3c. Where the distribution is centred. In Amendment 26 the discretized normal
is centred at the prediction and then reweighted by w. Reweighting moves the
mean and median off the centre (decision 17 already notes that the SD moves).
For test 5 this matters more than for the model: if the centre is the line but
the weighted median is not, the fair probability of "home wins by over
line - 0.5" is not what the line implies.

3d. Whether to correct the 2018 to 2024 means (-0.12 margin, +0.52 total).

3e. Whether sigma varies with the line. A larger |spread| or a higher total
could carry a wider spread of outcomes. Amendment 26 fixes sigma per season
for the model.

3f. **A break in nflverse's lines in 2025 (new finding).** 2025 total_line is
X.5 in all 285 games, and 2026 in all 62 finished so far. In 2018 to 2024,
119 to 166 totals per season were whole numbers. 2025 whole-number spreads
are 71 of 285, against 136 to 145 per season before. This suggests nflverse's
line source or capture rule changed in 2025. Why is **unverified**: the repo
holds nothing on it, and the network was not used. If the source changed,
2018 to 2024 residuals may not describe how 2025 lines miss, and "the close"
may mean something different in 2025.

### Options

- **A. Amendment 26's form with line residuals.** Centre the margin at
  spread_line and the total at total_line. sigma_M = 12.8897 and
  sigma_T = 13.1802 (pooled RMS, 2018 to 2024). w from 2018 to 2024 actual
  margins (the table above), or 2013 to 2024 to copy Amendment 26 exactly.
  Totals get no weights. One sigma for every 2025 game. Simple, and close to
  what the model would have used, but the median-offset problem of 3c remains.
- **B. As A, but re-centred.** For each game, choose the centre so that the
  weighted distribution's P(margin > line) equals P(margin < line), with
  pushes excluded on whole-number lines; the total is unchanged. This keeps
  the meaning "the line is the 50/50 point", which is what a line is. It costs
  one root-find per game, fixed in code.
- **C. Plain discretized normal, no w.** Fewest choices. It misprices strikes
  next to 3 and 7, exactly where Amendment 26 decision 15 says the normal
  fails.

**Recommendation: B, with sigma_M and sigma_T as the pooled 2018 to 2024
RMS (12.8897 and 13.1802), w from the same 2018 to 2024 games, no mean
correction (3d), and one sigma for all games (3e).**

- Reason for B: under the line-as-fair-value premise, the line is a median;
  only B keeps that.
- Reason for the window: 2018 to 2024 is the window tests 2 and 3 used, it
  never touches 2025, and it is already computed. 2013 to 2024 would copy
  Amendment 26 but brings in older scoring patterns for no gain.
- Also record, as a known limit, the 2025 line break (3f) and that sigma is
  not re-estimated on 2025. The amendment fixes these numbers as constants in
  code, with a test that recomputes them from the pinned schedules file.

## 4. The Kalshi price at the cutoff

### Facts

- The collector records fee-inclusive yes_ask and no_ask
  (kalshi_lines.parse_market_row), not bids, not mid, not volume.
- Candles have yes_bid and yes_ask (open, high, low, close), trade price
  (open, high, low, close, mean, previous), volume and open_interest.
- There is no "no" side in candles: no ask = 1 - yes bid.
- Intervals are 1, 60 or 1440 minutes; at most 5,000 candles per request.
- Field names differ by route:
  - historical (markets settled before 2026-08-05, so all of 2025):
    `/historical/markets/{ticker}/candlesticks`, with plain names (`close`,
    `volume`);
  - live: `/series/{series}/markets/{ticker}/candlesticks` and the batch
    route, with suffixed names (`close_dollars`, `volume_fp`).
- Quiet markets have gaps in the 1-minute series.
- Our snapshots matched 1-minute candles exactly on 97.9% of yes asks
  (2026-10-04). Mismatches came from price moves inside the snapshot minute.
- The cutoff rule is 8pm Eastern two calendar days before the game date, or
  Wednesday 8pm for Thursday games (Decision cutoff section, Amendments 1 and
  4).
- The cutoff table, data/historical/derived/game_times.parquet (column
  cutoff_et, built by features/game_times.py), covers **2010 to 2024 only**.
  No 2025 cutoff table exists.

### Ambiguities

4a. Which price: ask (fee-inclusive), bid, mid, last trade or candle close.

4b. Interval, and what "the price at the cutoff" means for a candle that ends
at end_period_ts. Is a candle's close the quote at end_period_ts?
**Unverified.** The 20:00 Eastern cutoff is on the hour, so a 60-minute
candle can end exactly at it, if hourly candles are aligned to the UTC hour.
Daily candles end at 04:00 UTC, which suggests hour alignment, but that is
**unverified**.

4c. No candle at the cutoff minute: carry the last candle forward (how far?),
skip the strike, or use the hourly candle.

4d. Units and names. The historical and live routes use different field names.
Whether historical values are in cents or dollars is not recorded in the repo
(**unverified**). The backfill must normalise both to dollars and test it.

4e. The 2025 cutoff table. It must be built for 2025 (and later 2026) with the
same rule. features/game_times.py has the rule, but its SEASONS stops at 2024.
Changing it changes a file under features/, which is in the hashed code set
(Amendment 32 decision 3), while the kill rule says "no more features are
built". A cutoff table is not a model feature, but the amendment should say so.

4f. Strikes listed only after the cutoff, and strikes with status other than
active at the cutoff.

### Options for the price

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| A. Fee-inclusive ask on the side bought: YES at yes_ask close + fee; NO at (1 - yes_bid close) + fee. Last 1-minute candle with end_period_ts at or before the cutoff, carried forward at most 60 minutes, else the strike has no price (counted, not bet). | What a taker would actually pay; matches the collector and clv_tracker; matches test 7's live practice. | 1-minute fetches for every strike of 258 games; gaps need a rule. |
| B. As A, but from the 60-minute candle ending exactly at the cutoff; fall back to A's 1-minute rule only if that candle is missing. | One candle per strike at the cutoff; cutoff on the hour; fewer requests; the hourly series also gives the volume for topic 7. | Relies on unverified hour alignment and on the close being the quote at end_period_ts. |
| C. Mid ((bid + ask) / 2) plus fee, or the last trade. | Less sensitive to a wide spread. | Not a price anyone can trade at; the last trade may be hours old and on either side. Overstates any edge. |

**Recommendation: A**, with B's hourly candle fetched as well, but only to
check A on a few markets (whether the two give the same quote at the cutoff)
and for volume.

- Gap rule: carry forward at most 60 minutes. Docs/data_sources.md's one
  checked gap was flat, but that is one case.
- Field names: normalise both routes to one schema in the backfill (topic 11).
- 2025 cutoffs: build them in new code outside features/, reusing
  features/game_times.cutoff by import, so that features/ and its hashes stay
  untouched. A test checks that the new table equals game_times.parquet's
  cutoff_et for 2010 to 2024.

Reason: the fee-inclusive ask is the only price a strategy can actually get.
Every lenient choice would turn a fee-sized edge into an apparent one.

### Cutoff timing of our own collector (relevant to options C and D of topic 1, and to test 7)

These are timestamps only, for the 87 games with game date up to 2026-11-09:

- No game has a snapshot within 60 minutes before its cutoff.
- 73 have one at or before the cutoff, almost all exactly 299.9 minutes
  earlier (the 15:00 ET quiet-day slot), and at most 484 minutes earlier.
- 14 games (all on 2026-10-04) have none, because the collector started at

dispatch.py's slots are hourly on game days and 09:00, 15:00 and 21:00 ET on
quiet days. There is no 20:00 ET slot on the cutoff day.

## 5. Fees

What the repo shows:

- `KALSHI_TAKER_FEE_RATE = 0.07`. fee = 0.07 x p x (1 - p), added to the raw
  ask (fee_inclusive_price), stored to 4 decimals.
- The code comment says it was checked on 2026-07-03 against one real fill
  ($10.00 / 21.39 contracts, raw ask 0.4502, implied rate 0.07) and against
  Kalshi's UI at three prices.
- The same fee is applied to no_ask.
- Candles are pre-fee: docs/data_sources.md section 4 applied the fee to the
  candles before matching our snapshots.
- Fixtures show `price_level_structure: linear_cent`, step 0.01 (2026 markets).

**Unverified (the repo does not show any of this):**

- Rounding. Whether Kalshi rounds the fee per order or per contract, up or to
  the nearest cent, and on what quantity. The 4-decimal per-contract fee in
  the code is not rounded at all. Rounding up per order can matter a lot for
  small orders.
- Whether the 0.07 rate applied to the NFL series throughout 2025, and
  whether Kalshi changed its fee schedule during 2025 or 2026.
- Whether the 2026-07-03 fill was on an NFL market. (It predates every NFL
  date in the repo; it may come from another sport.)
- Maker fees, and whether a resting (maker) order would pay less. Test 5
  assumes taker fills only.
- Any settlement or withdrawal fee.
- Whether the fee formula is the same on the NO side at price 1 - yes_bid.
- Price tick size in 2025 (the fixtures are 2026).

Options:

- **A. The repo's formula** per contract, unrounded, on both sides.
- **B. The repo's formula, rounded up to the next cent per contract.** This
  is the worst case for a one-contract order.
- **C. Rounded per order** at a stated order size (needs the size, topic 6).

**Recommendation: A as the primary, B reported beside it as a sensitivity.**
The amendment should list the unverified points above. Reason: A is the
formula the live system already uses and was checked against a fill. B shows
whether the result hangs on rounding, which is unverified. The sensitivity is
fixed now, so it is not a choice made after seeing results.

## 6. What counts as a bet

### Ambiguities

6a. Edge = fair probability - fee-inclusive price, for YES and NO on each
strike. Threshold: no number is in the rules. config.py's
MIN_EDGE_THRESHOLD = 0.03 is labelled "PLACEHOLDER — revisit after backtest".

6b. Correlated strikes. One game has many nested strikes (a SPREAD event has
about 25 tickers: 2,567 tickers across 101 events locally). "Home wins by
over 3.5" and "home wins by over 6.5" are almost the same bet.

6c. Stake: one contract, one dollar of cost, or Kelly.

6d. The unit of the "few hundred" count: strikes, game-markets or games. The
value is open (the "Few hundred flagged bets" ambiguity).

6e. A probability band. clv_tracker refuses prices at or below 0.02 and at or
above 0.98. Tail strikes are where a normal error spread is least trustworthy.

### Options

| Option | Bets per game-market | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Every strike and side with edge above the threshold | many | Most bets. | Bets are nested and highly correlated. The count overstates the evidence. "Few hundred" is met by duplication. |
| B. At most one per game and market: the strike and side with the largest edge above the threshold | 0 or 1 (at most 516 in 2025) | The count means something; errors are close to independent across games. | Throws away information; picks the largest edge, which is also the most likely to be a model error. |
| C. One per game and market at the strike nearest the line (the main line), bet if its edge clears the threshold | 0 or 1 | Most liquid strike; least dependent on w and sigma. | Ignores strikes where any mispricing may sit. |

Stake options:

- flat $1 of cost per bet;
- flat 1 contract;
- fractional Kelly. Kelly needs a trusted fair probability, which is the thing
  under test.

Threshold options:

- 0 (any positive edge after fee);
- a fixed 0.03;
- a fixed primary of 0.03 plus a reported-only grid (0, 0.02, 0.05).

**Recommendation:**

- **B**, plus C reported beside it as a sensitivity;
- band: only strikes with fair probability between 0.10 and 0.90, and price
  inside clv_tracker's (0.02, 0.98);
- flat $1 of cost per bet (ROI = profit / cost);
- primary threshold 0.03, with the grid reported but deciding nothing;
- **minimum 300 bets** in the primary rule (spread and total pooled) before
  any ROI is read. Below 300, the result is recorded as "not readable" and
  nothing is concluded.

Reason: B makes each bet roughly one independent game-market, so 300 means
300 pieces of evidence. 300 is the low end of "a few hundred" that 2025 can
still reach (516 possible). A higher minimum could make test 5 unreadable by
construction. Whether 2025 reaches 300 is itself a count that combines prices
and lines, so it is **not computed here**. It is reported at run time.

## 7. Liquidity gate

What the rules say: test 6 checks bid-ask width and volume on 2025 markets
"before looking at any model output". Thresholds and what happens when markets
are too thin are both open. 2025 market liquidity is an original open question.

Ambiguities:

- 7a. Which width: raw yes_ask - yes_bid at the cutoff, at the strike that
  would be bet.
- 7b. Which volume: contracts traded before the cutoff (cumulative), in the 24
  hours before it, or open interest at the cutoff.
- 7c. Per-strike filter or season-level go/no-go.
- 7d. Depth. Candles have no size at the ask. Whether a $1 order (or any
  size) would fill at the quoted ask cannot be recovered for 2025
  (**unverified and unrecoverable**; the market snapshot's yes_ask_size_fp
  exists only live).

Options:

- **A. Per-strike eligibility only.** A strike is tradable at the cutoff if
  its width is at most 0.05 and it traded at least once in the 24 hours
  before. Others are not bet and are counted.
- **B. Season-level gate only.** If the median width of main-line strikes at
  the cutoff is above 0.04, or fewer than half of the game-markets have any
  volume before the cutoff, test 5 stops and reports "too thin". No ROI.
- **C. Both,** run as a separate first stage.

**Recommendation: C**, with A's per-strike rule and B's season gate. The
gate's numbers are fixed in the amendment before anything is computed.

How and when the numbers are computed:

- In stage 1 of the test 5 run, after the backfill.
- From backfilled candles only: widths, volumes and counts per series and
  week. No line, score, settlement or fair value is read.
- Written to its own output file. Recorded in an amendment before stage 2
  (edges and results) runs.
- If the gate fails, stage 2 does not run, and test 7 is redesigned or
  dropped by amendment.

**These numbers are not computed in this review.**

## 8. What is reported

Ambiguities:

- 8a. The rules name ROI, CLV and calibration (test 6). Test 5 names none.
- 8b. How uncertainty is judged.
- 8c. "Ceiling", and what a pass or a fail means for tests 6 and 7.

Options:

- **A. ROI only,** with a one-sided test of mean profit per $1 > 0.
- **B. ROI, CLV and calibration,** each with a stated role.
  - ROI is the decision quantity.
  - CLV is the bet's fee-inclusive price against Kalshi's own last pre-kickoff
    price on the same side. The closing price needs a backfilled candle at
    kickoff, with kickoff from nflverse gameday and gametime in Eastern time.
  - Calibration of the fair probabilities against outcomes, in 10 fixed bins
    over all eligible strikes, not only bets. It checks topic 3's mapping,
    with no Kalshi price.
- **C. B plus Kalshi's own calibration** at the cutoff, descriptive.

**Recommendation: B.** Decision: ROI per $1 on the primary rule. Standard
error clustered by season-week, using the same formula as Amendment 38
decision 5, with G the number of 2025 weeks that have a bet. One-sided test
at 5%, with the 95% interval reported. CLV and calibration are descriptive.
Reason: one decision number keeps the one-shot honest, and season-week
clusters handle same-week dependence as test 3 did.

### Reading as a ceiling

nflverse's 2025 line was captured after the cutoff, probably near kickoff
(**unverified**, data_sources section 5). It knows injury and weather news
that arrived after the cutoff. Any "edge" therefore mixes Kalshi mispricing
with line movement after the cutoff. Test 5 is a ceiling on what a
decision-time line could do.

- **Fail** (ROI not significantly above 0, or below the 300-bet minimum, or the
  liquidity gate fails): even a hindsight close cannot beat Kalshi's price
  plus fee at the cutoff.
  - Test 6 is already spent (topic 1).
  - Test 7 under Kalshi-versus-close has no basis. The amendment should say
    in advance that test 7 then does not run as a betting test; at most it
    logs paper prices for information.
- **Pass:** necessary, not sufficient. It shows that the price at the cutoff
  and the later close differ by more than the fee, but not that anyone could
  know the close at the cutoff.
  - Test 7 must show it with a line that exists at decision time (topic 9).
  - A pass carries no ROI claim of its own.

## 11. The Kalshi backfill (agenda item 7)

What it must provide, by topic:

- **Series (topic 2):** KXNFLSPREAD and KXNFLTOTAL for 2025 (and KXNFLGAME
  only if ML is kept). First-half series are not needed.
- **Market records:** ticker, event_ticker, floor_strike, strike_type, status,
  open_time, close_time, result, settlement time, rules_primary,
  rules_secondary, yes_sub_title. These serve topics 3, 4f and 10.
- **Candles (topics 4, 7, 8):**
  - 1-minute candles from 120 minutes before the cutoff to the cutoff, for the
    gap rule and the price;
  - 60-minute candles over the market's whole life, for volume before the
    cutoff, the hourly cross-check and the price at kickoff (CLV);
  - fields: end_period_ts, yes_bid OHLC, yes_ask OHLC, trade price OHLC,
    volume, open_interest.
- **Routes and names:** the historical route for every 2025 market (settled
  before 2026-08-05). One normalised schema mapping `close` and
  `close_dollars`, and `volume` and `volume_fp`, with units confirmed (cents
  against dollars, 4d) and tested on fixtures saved from both routes.
- **Fee step:** none. The backfill stores raw prices. The fee is applied once,
  in test 5's code, so it is never doubled (the collector's comment warns
  about this).
- **Game match:** nflverse game_id to Kalshi event on season and teams, with
  week taken from nflverse.
  - Not the ticker date: Kalshi keeps a moved game's original date.
  - (season, away, home) is unique among 2025 REG games (0 duplicates), so
    the match key is unique. The ticker date may only confirm the match
    within a window (for example ±7 days), never decide it.
  - Team codes: Rams `LA` in 2025 Kalshi tickers, `LAR` in 2026; `JAC` for
    Jacksonville; Washington `WAS`. nflverse uses `LA`, `JAX`, `WAS`.
- **Known match misses (KXNFLGAME, data_sources section 2):**
  - the two moved week 18 games (26JAN04CARTB, 26JAN04SEASF), which are
    Saturday games and not version 1;
  - NE@DEN conference game, which has no KXNFLGAME event;
  - the Super Bowl;
  - plus the SF@SEA divisional pair of events.
  None of these is a version 1 game. The spread and total match rate is
  **unverified**. The backfill must report it, and must stop if any of the
  258 version 1 games has no unique spread or total event. Preseason events
  are dropped.
- **Output:** git-ignored, under data/historical/ (already ignored). For
  example data/historical/kalshi_2025/: the raw JSON responses as received,
  one normalised parquet for markets and one for candles, and a manifest with
  SHA-256, row counts, request times and the API base URL. Test 5 then reads
  only the parquet files, by hash.
- **Network and approval:** the backfill is the only step that uses the
  network. It runs once, after its code and tests are committed and after you
  approve the run. It reads no nflverse line or score except game_id, season,
  week, teams and gameday for matching.
