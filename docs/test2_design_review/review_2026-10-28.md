## 1. Prediction error metric

**Already fixed**
- Original rules, test 2 (lines 60–64): choices are made by "smallest prediction error, not profit". No Kalshi data and no 2025 data are used.
- Scope (line 10): the model predicts home and away scores for the full game and the first half. One shared model serves all six markets.
- Discipline (lines 89–90): first-half markets are judged on score accuracy against simple baselines plus forward data.
- Amendments 6 and 8 (ablation ladder) and Amendment 11 decision "Test 2 runs once with these two features" (key starters) use the same metric. Amendment 11's open questions say the metric "stay[s] open".

**Ambiguities**
- 1a. The error function is not named (squared, absolute or another).
- 1b. The targets are not named: home and away points, or margin and total. Under squared error with equal weights the two give the same answer: errors e_m = e_h − e_a and e_t = e_h + e_a give e_m² + e_t² = 2(e_h² + e_a²), so the choice matters only for absolute error or unequal weights. Under absolute error it does change the ranking.
- 1c. Whether the first half enters the selection metric, and with what weight. The Discipline line points to first-half accuracy being judged separately, against baselines.
- 1d. How seasons combine: every game weighted equally across 2018 to 2024, or the mean of 7 season errors.
- 1e. Whether overtime points count in the full-game target. The schedule final includes overtime, and 170 version 1 games had it.
- 1f. What "one shared model for all six markets" means for the first half. Options are a separate first-half fit on the same features, a first-half prediction equal to a share of the full-game prediction, or a joint fit. This is listed again as item O3.

**Options**
- A. MSE on full-game home and away points (equivalently margin and total), equal weight per game, pooled over 2018 to 2024. First half reported but not used to select.
- B. A, plus first-half MSE added with a fixed weight (for example 0.5).
- C. MAE on margin and total, summed.
- D. A probability score (log loss or CRPS) on strike outcomes. This needs the error spread fixed first (item 9).

**Recommendation: A.** Squared error is what the ridge fits minimize. The margin and total equivalence removes one choice. Equal weight per game makes the paired per-game differences of item 2 natural. Keeping the first half out of selection follows the Discipline line. Overtime should be included, because the full-game markets settle on the final score, but that must be stated.

## 2. Near-tie tolerance; "simpler" against "middle"

**Already fixed**
- Original rules (line 63): near-ties go to "the simpler or middle option". Ambiguity list (lines 114–117): there is no tolerance, and nothing says which wins in a conflict.
- Amendment 6: in the QB ablation the run without the term is the simpler option. The term stays only if it wins by more than the tolerance.
- Amendment 8: the three-run nested ladder, tested in order (passing, then rushing). Simpler wins near-ties.
- Amendment 11: key starters are kept only if the run with them wins by more than the tolerance. The tolerance is "set by its own amendment before test 2 runs".
- Amendment 22 decision 3: penalty tuning uses a one-standard-error rule on paired per-game differences, with a grid-position-sum tie-break. Amendment 23 decision 3 records the cost of that tie-break: it chose 100/3000 over the best 300/300. It adds that a future rule "should require the winner to be at least as large as the best in both penalties".

**Ambiguities**
- 2a. The tolerance: its size and its type (relative, absolute or standard error).
- 2b. Whether Amendment 22's one-standard-error rule is reused, and its exact form for a per-game metric. Under equal weight per game the standard error of a mean difference is sd(d_g)/√n, with n = 1,767. Amendment 22 instead divides by the total play count.
- 2c. Whether the standard error should allow for correlation among games in the same week or season.
- 2d. "Simpler" for each choice. Fixed: the ladder (fewer QB terms) and key starters (off). Not fixed:
  - lookback: shorter, longer, or no "simpler" at all;
  - half-life: is a longer half-life, closer to equal weights, simpler?
  - F: is a shorter fade (less prior) simpler?
  - form: is additive simpler? It has no middle.
- 2e. "Middle" exists only for the 3-level choices (lookback 5, half-life 8, F 8).
- 2f. When several combinations tie in a joint search, which dimension's tie-break applies first (lexicographic order).
- 2g. Whether a tie-break may pick an option that is farther from the best than another tied option, the Amendment 23 lesson.
- 2h. The ladder and key starters rules say "smaller error by more than the tolerance". Whether that tolerance is the same one used for near-ties among lookback, half-life and F.

**Options**
- A. Reuse Amendment 22 adapted per game: an option ties with the best if its mean error minus the best's is at most one standard error of the paired per-game differences. Ordinal 3-level choices go to the middle. Binary choices go to the simpler option (form: additive). Tie-breaks may only move toward the middle or the simpler option from the best, never past it.
- B. A relative tolerance (for example 0.1% of the best MSE), the original Amendment 9 rule, with the same tie-break order.
- C. A paired significance test (one-sided t or Diebold-Mariano at 5%). An option is kept only if it beats the simpler one significantly.
- D. Two standard errors instead of one, which is more conservative and keeps fewer terms.

**Recommendation: A.** It is the rule the project already uses, and its noise scale fits 1,767 paired games. Fix one order for conflicts, for example:
1. ladder and key starters by Amendments 6, 8 and 11 (simpler wins);
2. 3-level choices to the middle;
3. form to additive.

Add the Amendment 23 safeguard: a tie-break may only choose among tied options that are at least as simple, or as central, as the best in every dimension.

## 3. How test 2's choices are searched

**Already fixed**
- Original rules (line 61): train on the previous 3, 5 or 8 seasons.
- Amendment 5: half-life grid 4, 8, 16; F grid 4, 8, 12.
- Amendment 9: penalties are not chosen in test 2. Test 2 chooses lookback, half-life, F, form, the ladder and key starters.
- Amendment 8: the ladder is a ladder, not a grid. Amendments 6 and 11: each ablation is "one extra comparison, not a new grid".
- Amendments 23 and 25: penalties and c are fixed per half-life and run.

**Count**
- Full grid: 3 lookbacks × 3 half-lives × 3 F × 2 forms × 3 ladder runs × 2 key-starter settings = **324 configurations**. That is 2,268 score-model fits over 7 seasons, before any regularization grid or inner folds.
- Feature-table settings: half-life × F × run = 27 builds, 54 tables with key starters on and off. Lookback and form need no new tables.
- An example fixed order:
  1. A grid of 3 × 3 × 3 × 2 = 54 configurations at the full run with key starters on.
  2. The ladder at the chosen setting: 2 more runs.
  3. Key starters off: 1 more.

  That totals **57 configurations** and 399 fits.

**Ambiguities**
- 3a. Full grid or fixed order. The "not a new grid" wording of Amendments 6, 8 and 11 points to a fixed order but doesn't say so for the other choices.
- 3b. If ordered: what is searched first, and at which base setting (for example, the ladder at the full run or at the grid's winner).
- 3c. Whether the ladder and key starters are re-evaluated after the grid choice.
- 3d. One choice for all of 2018 to 2024, or one per season. Per season is only meaningful if each season's choice uses earlier seasons (item 4).
- 3e. Whether the score model is refit only once per test season (my reading of line 61) or also within the season.
- 3f. Whether the regularization strength (item 6) is a seventh search dimension.

**Options**
- A. Full grid of 324, one pooled choice, with the tie-break order of item 2.
- B. Fixed order of 57: a grid of lookback, half-life, F and form at full run with key starters on, then the ladder, then key starters. One pooled choice.
- C. B, but the ladder and key starters come first, at the middle of every grid, and then the 54-cell grid runs at the chosen run.
- D. Either A or B, with choices made walk-forward per season (item 4, option B).

**Recommendation: B.** It follows "not a new grid", gives one fifth the fits of A, and tests fewer configurations, which lowers the selection bias of item 4. With the full grid, many configurations sit within one standard error, so the tie-break matters more than the data. State that the model is refit once per test season.

## 4. Selection bias into test 3

**Already fixed**
- Test 3 (lines 65–68): regress actual margin and total on the close plus the model prediction, over 2018 to 2024. The kill rule (lines 82–83) depends on test 3.
- Discipline (line 87): any change after seeing validation results is a new version.
- Amendment 12 decision 20 handles frozen settings fit on earlier data, and says no scored game is affected.

Test 2's choice is a new overlap: settings chosen on 2018 to 2024 errors, and test 3 then scores the same seasons.

**Ambiguities**
- 4a. Whether test 3 uses the chosen configuration's 2018 to 2024 predictions as they are.
- 4b. Whether test 3's claim must be out of sample with respect to the selection.
- 4c. Which predictions test 2 saves for test 3: the chosen configuration only, or all of them.

**Options and costs**
- A. **Pooled choice, test 3 on the same seasons.** Costs no seasons. Test 3 can only claim information "after in-sample selection among N configurations". The model coefficient is biased upward, more so with 324 configurations than with 57. Its standard error understates the uncertainty.
- B. **Walk-forward selection.** Season T's settings are chosen only from test 2 errors of seasons 2018 to T−1. 2018 has no earlier result, so it either uses a default fixed by amendment now (the middle or simpler option everywhere) or is dropped from test 3. 2019's choice rests on one season, which is noisy. Cost: 0 seasons with a default, or 1 season (249 games) if 2018 is dropped. Test 3 can claim every prediction is out of sample for its own selection. The claim is about the selection procedure, not about one fixed configuration, because settings may differ by season. Test 2 must save all configurations' predictions, or at least those of the chosen path.
- C. **Split seasons.** Select on 2018 to 2020 (or 2018 to 2021), and run test 3 on 2021 to 2024 (1,026 games) or 2022 to 2024 (764 games). Cost: test 3 loses 3 or 4 of its 7 seasons, which lowers its power. Its claim is clean for one fixed configuration.
- D. **Pre-registered primary model.** Fix one configuration now (middle or simpler on every choice) as test 3's primary model, and run it on all 7 seasons. The test 2 winner is reported as secondary. Cost: no seasons, but the primary model may be worse than the selected one. Its claim is clean.
- E. **Select before 2018.** Use 2016 and 2017 as selection seasons. Cost: the lookback options are nearly the same there, because the 2013 floor makes L=5 and L=8 identical before 2019. This also changes the rule that test 2 runs on 2018 to 2024.

**Recommendation: B, with a 2018 default fixed now.** It keeps all 7 seasons in test 3 and makes every prediction out of sample with respect to selection. It also matches "walk-forward" in the rules. Its final selection, over 2018 to 2024, is the natural setting for the 2025 holdout. Pair it with item 3 option B, to reduce the noise of early per-season choices. D is the cheaper fallback if per-season settings seem undesirable.

## 5. Additive and multiplicative forms as equations

**Already fixed**
- Features (lines 33–34): "Model form tests multiplicative (plays times points per play) against additive."
- Pace is offensive kept plays per game, recency weighted, not opponent adjusted and not blended (Amendments 5, 7 and 8; Amendment 12 decision 6). It excludes garbage-time plays.
- Amendment 11: the key-starter features are oriented to the team, with "Each [having] its own learned coefficient, shared across home and away". This implies a stacked team-game model, at least for those terms.
- Version 1 has no neutral-site games (Amendment 16 decision 2 check).

**Notation.** The model has one row per team i in a game against opponent j.
- y_i: team i's points (full game; the first half uses the same form).
- H_i: 1 if i is at home, 0 if away.
- off_i: i's 4 offense ratings (pass_epa_off, pass_success_off, rush_epa_off, rush_success_off).
- def_j: j's 4 defense ratings (pass_epa_def, pass_success_def, rush_epa_def, rush_success_def).
- pace_i and pace_j.
- qp_i = qb_pass_i, EPA per dropback scale. qr_i = qb_rush_i, EPA per game (Amendment 10).
- k1_i = own_off_value_out_i and k2_i = opp_def_value_out_i.
- s_i: schedule features: rest_i, short_week_i, bye_i, tz_shift_i; possibly rest_j and the opponent's flags; primetime, div_game, roof (dome and retractable as dummies, outdoors as base). tz_shift is 0 for the home team.

**Additive (A1):**
y_i = α + η·H_i + Σ_k β_k·off_{k,i} + Σ_k δ_k·def_{k,j} + γ₁·pace_i + γ₂·pace_j + λ₁·qp_i + λ₂·qr_i + κ₁·k1_i + κ₂·k2_i + ρ·s_i + ε_i

**Multiplicative, with a fixed exposure (M1),** linear in its parameters, so ridge works as is:
y_i = pace_i × (θ₀ + θ_H·H_i + Σ_k θ_k·off_{k,i} + Σ_k φ_k·def_{k,j} + μ₁·qp_i + κ₁·k1_i + κ₂·k2_i + ψ·s_i) + λ₂·qr_i + ε_i

The regressors are pace_i times each per-play input. qr_i stays outside the product because it is already per game.

**Multiplicative, with fitted plays (M2),** a bilinear least-squares problem with no closed form:
y_i = (π₀ + π₁·pace_i + π₂·pace_j) × (θ₀ + θ_H·H_i + θ·off_i + φ·def_j + μ₁·qp_i + κ·k_i + ψ·s_i) + λ₂·qr_i + ε_i

**Multiplicative, log link (M3),** a Poisson or Gaussian model with log link and an offset, fit iteratively:
log E[y_i] = log(pace_i) + θ₀ + θ_H·H_i + θ·off_i + φ·def_j + μ₁·qp_i + μ₂·qr_i + κ·k_i + ψ·s_i

**Ambiguities**
- 5a. Which form (M1, M2, M3 or another) "plays times points per play" means.
- 5b. Which plays term: own pace only, own and opponent pace, or their mean. Pace counts only kept plays, so it is a proxy for plays, not total plays.
- 5c. Which inputs sit inside the points-per-play factor and which stay outside it. qb_rush is per game, and the schedule flags and key-starter values have no natural per-play unit.
- 5d. Whether the opponent's QB, pace or key starters enter team i's equation.
- 5e. Stacked team-game rows with shared coefficients, or separate home and away equations. Amendment 11 implies shared coefficients for key starters.
- 5f. How home field enters: H_i, or separate intercepts for home and away points.
- 5g. Which schedule columns are inputs. short_week is a function of rest, which raises collinearity. group (Thursday, Sunday, Monday) is in the table but not in the Features list. tz_shift is always 0 for the home team.
- 5h. Whether the zero-fill flags and P are inputs. Amendment 16 decision 1c says the table holds "model inputs, ids and flags", so not every column is an input.
- 5i. Whether margin and total come from the two team equations (y_home − y_away and y_home + y_away).

**Options:** A1 against M1; A1 against M3; A1 against M2.

**Recommendation: A1 against M1.** Both are linear in their parameters and fit by the same closed-form ridge, so the comparison isolates the form. Fix the inside and outside split of M1 (5c) and the input list (5g, 5h) in the same amendment.

## 6. Score model regularization

**Already fixed**
- Features (line 49): "Regularization on all inputs." Ambiguity list (lines 148–149): the type and strength are not specified.
- Amendment 9 fixes the rating ridges only (units, unpenalized intercept and home term). It says nothing about the score model.

**Ambiguities**
- 6a. The type (ridge, lasso, elastic net).
- 6b. Which coefficients are penalized: does "all inputs" include the intercept and the home term? Amendment 9 leaves those unpenalized in the rating fits.
- 6c. Whether inputs are standardized, and on which rows. They should be standardized on training rows only.
- 6d. How the strength is chosen:
  - fixed now;
  - by cross-validation inside the training window (leave one season out, or walk forward inside the window);

- 6f. The strength grid, and the tie rule for it (the item 2 rule or larger-is-simpler).

**Recommendation: A.** It uses only training seasons and gives the same closed-form solve as the rating fits. It doesn't add to the test 2 search or to item 4's bias. It also suits M1. M3 would need an iterative solver.

## 9. Error spread for strike probabilities

**Already fixed**
- Scope (line 11): "using the model's own historical error spread". Ambiguity list (lines 126–127): the form, and per market or shared, are not given.
- Amendment 12 lists the error spread under "Later", not "Before test 2".

**Ambiguities**
- 9a. The distribution form: normal, Student t, empirical, or discrete with key-number mass at 3 and 7.
- 9b. Its variables: margin and total separately, or home and away jointly (bivariate with a correlation). This affects joint markets.
- 9c. Per market or shared, and full game against first half.
- 9d. Which residuals estimate it: in-sample training residuals (optimistic, too narrow) or out-of-sample walk-forward residuals from earlier test seasons. The latter means 2018 has none.
- 9e. Whether its parameters may vary with the prediction (for example, the total's spread scaling with the predicted total).
- 9f. Whether it must be fixed before test 2. Test 2 selects on point error, so the spread doesn't enter selection under item 1 option A. But the form would be chosen after test 2 residuals exist, so a form chosen later could be shaped by them. If item 1 option D is chosen, the spread enters test 2 directly and must be fixed first.

**Options**
- A. Fix now: normal on margin and on total separately, full game and first half separately. Each standard deviation is estimated from out-of-sample residuals of earlier seasons only; 2018 uses a stated training-residual rule.
- B. Fix now: empirical residual distribution with integer smoothing, estimated as in A.
- C. Bivariate normal on home and away points, with a correlation, estimated as in A.
- D. Defer the form to after test 2, accepting that residuals may shape it. The rules allow this ("Later"), but Amendment 1's spirit and Discipline line 87 argue against it.

**Recommendation: fix the form before test 2 runs (A, or B if key numbers matter).** The parameters are then the only thing estimated from residuals, and only from earlier seasons. This costs nothing now and removes the risk in 9f.

## 10. Rows with missing P (Amendment 14)

**Already fixed**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 253 | 11 | 11 | before 2015 |
| 2014 | 250 | 20 | 20 | before 2015 |
| 2015–2024 | 2,504 | 0 | 0 | none |

That is 31 games and 31 team rows; no game has both sides missing. The "empty pool" reason occurs 0 times. train_exclude equals "any side missing P" on every row. **Ambiguities**

**Recommendation: A, plus a stop if the count in 2018 to 2024 is not 0.** It closes the question at no cost under current data.

## 12. Week-1 primary QB source (Amendment 11 open question)

**Already fixed**
- Amendment 11: in a team's first game of a season, the primary QB is the QB with the most kept dropbacks for the team in its previous regular season. The returning-starter rule can override it. Amendment 14 decision 5 adds the tie-break.
- The open question is whether any roster or depth-chart source can be shown to be stamped before the cutoff. Starting QB ids of the predicted game are never model inputs and are allowed only in ceiling tests.

**Proposed diagnostic (not run)**
1. Population: every version 1 team-game that is the team's first regular-season game, 2013 to 2024. A row is a first game when g = 0; team_ratings.games_before computes g, or the schedule order gives it.
2. Default: home_primary_qb_id or away_primary_qb_id from the saved feature table. These ids are the same in every setting (Amendment 14 decision 6).
3. Actual: home_qb_id or away_qb_id of that same game in the schedules file. This is a hindsight read, used for this diagnostic only and never as an input.
4. Report: the disagreement count and rate overall and by season, split by primary_path (own or no_history) and by whether the returning-starter rule fired. For context, also report the rate for candidate rules that use only pre-cutoff facts: the last starter of the previous season (regular season or playoffs), and the leader over the team's last 16 finished regular-season games across seasons.
5. Inputs: no scores, no features other than the ids, no ratings.

**Trigger options (to be fixed before the diagnostic runs)**
- A. Overall disagreement above 15% opens a rule-change amendment.
- B. A candidate pre-cutoff rule lowers disagreement by at least 5 percentage points compared with the default, on 2013 to 2017 only. Test-season starters are then not used to design a feature.
- C. Any season above 25% disagreement.
- D. Report only. A change happens only if a source stamped before the cutoff is found (the literal open question).

**Ambiguities**
- 12a. The threshold and its unit.
- 12b. Whether test seasons may inform the rule.
- 12c. What the change would be, since no stamped source is known.
- 12d. Whether the diagnostic must run before test 2. Amendment 12 lists the first-game QB source under "Later".

**Recommendation: B.** It ties the trigger to an available alternative, which matters because no stamped source is known. Using 2013 to 2017 only keeps test-season hindsight out of the feature design. I don't know the likely disagreement rate.

## Run time (test 2 only)

**Feature tables (measured basis).** Test 1's 9 fresh full builds took 1,441.3 s at test 1 settings (docs/test1_results/full_timing.csv).
